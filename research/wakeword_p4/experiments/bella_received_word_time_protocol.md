# Reading a complete send with the first-open clock

A 100 ms pre-open audio crop removed many leading words from the transcript. On the 96 consumed music and panel interruptions it also lost complete commands that a reader had previously transcribed. Test whether a reader can use the entire encoded utterance as context, then omit words heard before that same clock. This is a receive-side English transcript experiment.

Use all 48 +2 dB Nyx and official Willow inputs from the 12 geometric rooms, including missed opens, and all 96 historical +2 dB Nyx interruptions. Use both archived P4 sender schedules. Reconstruct each delivered stream from the sent packets and require its PCM SHA256 to equal the full ASR input. Set the boundary to the documented 100 ms pre-open sample with the measured number of leading silence frames. Keep the full transcript from the first word whose timestamp midpoint falls on or after the boundary. If all midpoints precede the boundary, the output is empty. The known command, its onset, source stems and figures of merit play no part in this decision.

Use the archived Whisper turbo word timestamps. For Qwen3-ASR use its archived predicted English words with the public Qwen3 forced aligner on the RTX 4090. Align the lower-case alphabetic words used by the established scoring rule. Preserve the unfiltered predicted words and the estimated word intervals alongside the selected suffix. Score the frozen method against the full-send and audio-crop baselines by normalized whole-transcript match, complete command suffix, and gains and losses on the same inputs. Keep quiet and competing rooms, both addresses, and historical source levels identifiable. This is a follow-up on consumed synthetic cases.
