# Frozen generated wake sounds at two additional measured listener orientations

Use the IoSR Surrey Listening Room measurements at 30° and 300° head rotation from commit d36af1bbd0f4083a9f9ee678ff8f499044e851a7. Previous experiments used the 0° left ear. Use the right ear here, with the front-centre and back-centre loudspeakers (one-based channels 3 and 9). Use the same normalization for every response, derived from the historical 0° left-ear front-centre impulse. Preserve the relative levels and delays of the four new paths. Downsample each measured impulse from 48 to 16 kHz using the existing polyphase filter. Record the largest absolute sample in its first 50 ms as the direct-arrival clock estimate. [Dataset and license](https://github.com/IoSR-Surrey/IoSR_ListeningRoom_BRIRs/tree/d36af1bbd0f4083a9f9ee678ff8f499044e851a7)

Test the released 400 ms Nyx two-marker stimulus, its original +5 dB control, the paired +5 dB af_bella Willow address, and address-erased commands. Take the three nominal player pairs from scenes 077, 079 and 090. Separate the existing dry target from the interfering sentence using the released erased player track and the corresponding isolated command. For each orientation and target direction place the interferer on the opposite loudspeaker or silence it. Keep the playback clock, command samples and source levels. Use quarter gain at the ADC and common Gaussian noise of 12 counts RMS per geometry, generated by NumPy PCG64 seed 273961. The panel stays silent. Report clipping and first-open time against the command arrival estimate. Both the C161+marker receiver with the two recovery patches and official Hey Willow DEFAULT receive all 96 microphones, preserving cross-address and erased trials.

Read the received first commands at the native opening of each intended detector on the nominal 400 ms Nyx and Willow stimuli, under immediate and delayed service. Include missed wakes in the denominator. Use the original capture AEC/AGC, Opus, sender and both unprompted GPU readers. The source voices and building appeared in earlier experiments. The two right-ear orientation measurements are new development inputs for this frozen cue.
